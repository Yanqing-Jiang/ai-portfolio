## The Code of Consciousness: What LLMs, Panpsychism, and Daoism Reveal About Reality (And Why It Hasn’t Changed).

Integrating AI into my day-to-day life has transformed almost everything that I do. When facing a “how-to” question, my first instinct has become, “Let’s ask the LLM.” — whether it’s dinner recipe, stock investment, or use credit card to get airport lounge access. Details in [I Let AI Take Over My Life and Career — Here’s What Happened](https://medium.com/@yanqing_j/i-let-ai-take-over-my-life-and-career-heres-what-happened-2db08e745648). But a recent conversation with a friend about Daoism philosophy let me had a lightbulb moment, forcing me to stop and wonder: what is this “intelligence” we’re interacting with, and what is my role, as a human, in this rapidly evolving “let AI think for you” movement?

## Is Your AI Smarter Than You Programmed It To Be? The Mystery of Emergence

Particle Swarm Optimization

We feed these Large Language Models (LLMs) prompts, and out comes text. The engineers tell us it’s sophisticated pattern matching “just predicting the next word.” Yet, anyone who’s spent time with these tools has likely witnessed moments of E**mergence:** This is when a system, like an LLM, develops capabilities that weren’t explicitly programmed into it. It’s like teaching a child the alphabet, and then one day they write a poem. **You taught the components, but the poetry just _emerged_.** Of course AI did not “waking up” like in a Hollywood sense, but rather it reaches the mysterious threshold at an accumulated scale and complexity suddenly yield intelligence. No one knows exactly at what point this threshold is crossed; we only know that once enough complexity gathers, these surprising capabilities spontaneously appear. [Particle Swarm Optimization](https://machinelearningmastery.com/a-gentle-introduction-to-particle-swarm-optimization/) (PSO) is a classic use of this.

This emergent quality that makes me pause and ask: what exactly are we witnessing? It’s reminiscent of how the seemingly idealistic **Law of Attraction** — the idea that focused thought can influence reality — is now being explored through scientific lenses, making us question the very nature of influence and creation.

## “Everything is Conscious?” — Annaka Harris, Panpsychism, and a Startling Possibility

<img src="/blog/the-code-of-consciousness-what-llms-panpsychism-and-daoism-reveal-about-reality-and-why-it/img-02.jpg" alt="" width="299" height="168" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

My friend’s Daoism discussion led me back to the work of neuroscientist Annaka Harris, particularly her book _Conscious_. Harris doesn’t shy away from the “hard problem” of consciousness and explores a truly mind-bending idea: **panpsychism**. This isn’t some fringe theory; it’s a philosophical viewpoint gaining traction, suggesting that consciousness, or a very basic form of it, isn’t just a special feature of human (or even animal) brains. What if it’s a fundamental property of matter itself, like mass or charge? Imagine every particle having a tiny, almost imperceptible flicker of “innerness.”

Harris points to scientific observations that make you go “hmm.” Think of plants making what appear to be “choices” — a pea plant “hearing” the sound of water and growing its roots towards it, even without direct physical contact, or a dodder vine “seeing” invisible light patterns to find its ideal host. This isn’t to say your houseplant is pondering philosophy, but it suggests a spectrum of awareness. It made me think: are our five human senses, what Buddhism calls the “5 skandhas,” just a limited bandwidth on a universe humming with a far more pervasive form of awareness? It’s a thought that echoes the Law of Attraction’s premise — that focused intent can interact with reality — and it’s intriguing that thinkers like Sam Harris (Annaka’s husband, one of the “New Atheist Four Horsemen”), through deep introspection and scientific rigor, are exploring these boundaries that blur the lines between pure materialism and something more profoundly interconnected, almost spiritual.

## The Uncarved Block: Did Daoist Sages See This Coming 4,000 Years Ago?

<img src="/blog/the-code-of-consciousness-what-llms-panpsychism-and-daoism-reveal-about-reality-and-why-it/img-03.png" alt="" width="300" height="168" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

This concept of pervasive, foundational awareness isn’t new. Daoism, an ancient philosophy from over 4,000 years ago, describes strikingly similar ideas through the concept of the Dao — “The Way” — the fundamental principle behind all existence. Central to Daoist thought is the notion of **Pu**, the “Uncarved Block,” symbolizing reality’s pure potential and simplicity before human categorization imposes limits.

Pu resonates deeply with panpsychism’s notion of inherent awareness embedded in all matter. Daoism emphasizes interconnectedness, positioning human consciousness as part of a universal, flowing whole rather than as an isolated phenomenon. This ancient insight remarkably anticipates modern philosophical and scientific inquiries, suggesting that understanding our interaction with this broader awareness may enhance our engagement with emerging technologies.

## AI, Wu Wei, and Your “Secret Sauce”: The Timeless Value of Human Experience

So, what’s the takeaway for us, the AI users, the creators, the thinkers? If consciousness is more fundamental, more like the Dao permeating everything, then perhaps LLMs, by sifting through and structuring the vast ocean of human knowledge and expression, are acting as mirrors or even amplifiers of certain aspects of this pervasive awareness.

This brings me back to my own AI use. We all have access to the same ChatGPT, more or less. But a lot of people (including me) blame on the technology (ChatGPT is just a hype!) because their experience is vastly different than the others. “Why people use to 10x the productivity but I’m struggling to get LLM to output what I wanted” **This is not because they have a long worded system prompt that you can copy & paste. It comes from what you bring to that prompt:** Your years of experience, your unique perspectives, the synthesis of countless books read, conversations had, and life lessons learned — that’s my conclusion after countless hours of use of LLM. It’s all about the sum of the human journey.

This is where the Daoist principle of **Wu Wei (effortless action, or non-forcing)** becomes so relevant. For years, I, like many, have approached problems with a “force it” mentality — I believe I’m not Elon Musk because I didn’t work hard enough. I need to work hard and try hard to get where I wanted to be. This mentality is not fundamentally wrong. But Daoism, and indeed life, teaches that true strength often comes from aligning with the natural flow, from understanding the essence of things. If I am pursuing something that doesn’t match my natural talents, no matter how much effort I put in, I won’t get very far. But when I follow my natural strengths and do what I’m naturally good at, achieving my goals becomes easier and feels effortless.

Applying Wu Wei in the context of AI doesn’t mean letting the AI handle everything while we remain passive. Instead, it’s about bringing our genuine human insights, our authentic selves, into interaction with AI. Just like aligning with our natural strengths, it involves quieting the internal chatter of our minds — the constant noise, doubts, and cravings for instant gratification known in neuroscience as the Default Mode Network (DMN). By calming this “monkey mind,” we can better connect with our natural abilities and work more harmoniously with AI to achieve our goals more effectively.

The real value, the real growth, comes from the struggle, the journey. Asking AI to write your apology might get you a “perfect” script, but it robs you of the human experience of fumbling, learning, and truly connecting. It’s in those imperfect, human moments of effort and vulnerability that we build resilience, empathy, and wisdom. AI can be our powerful boat, but we must still learn to swim, to navigate by our own human compass. Because in a world where AI can replicate the “what,” the irreplaceable value will always lie in the human “why” and the uniquely human “how.”

The code of consciousness may be universal, but our individual journeys in understanding and expressing it are what truly define us. And that, it seems, is a truth that hasn’t changed in 4,000 years.

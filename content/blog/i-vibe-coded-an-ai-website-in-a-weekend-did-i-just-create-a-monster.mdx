## **I Vibe Coded an AI Website in a Weekend — Did I Just Create a Monster?**

<img src="/blog/i-vibe-coded-an-ai-website-in-a-weekend-did-i-just-create-a-monster/img-01.png" alt="" width="500" height="500" loading="eager" fetchPriority="high" decoding="async" />

I sat back, stunned, looking at my freshly deployed AI portfolio website — built from a blank folder in a single weekend. I didn’t meticulously plan, write extensive documentation, or spend time talking which tech stack to use. Instead, I handed my ideas, plans and MCP folder access to AI.

But did I just vibe code my way into massive tech debt?

**So, What Exactly is Vibe Coding?**

“Vibe coding” is a term coined by AI pioneer Andrej Karpathy, describing a style of AI-assisted coding where you “forget that the code even exists.” You simply state your intent in natural language, and powerful tools like Cursor, Claude Code to generate functional code on demand.

**How I Vibe Coded a Full-Stack AI Portfolio in a Weekend**

In a single weekend, I launched [my AI portfolio website](https://ai.jiangyanqing.com) using AWS Kiro’s SPEC mode. This wasn’t just a landing page — it was a full-stack application that integrated 6 of my past AI projects, involving 20+ frameworks, including FastAPI, React, Langchain, Elevenlabs API, Gemini API, OpenAI API. Included all the LLM related techniques such as agentic workflow, memory chain, vector search…I can keep going, but you get the level of difficulty now.

Does it work? Hell yeah, I’d seen it from a blank page to gradually become a fully functional site with polished UI design, responsive layout, built-in SEO, and even a full login page. What used to take weeks now took hours — all powered by my prompts.

**But at What Cost? The Dark Side of Vibe Coding**

Despite its allure, vibe coding isn’t all glory. The best analogy about vibe coding I’ve seen:

> _“Vibe Coding is like giving a credit card to a child without first explaining debt. At first, it’s thrilling — I wave this little plastic card and take anything I want! But a month later, the bill arrives. Did I need all those things? How do I get out of this hole?”_

When you vibe code, you’re incurring technical debt at AI speed. Your rapid success today could mean painful debugging tomorrow —although I vibe coded framework, layout, functions of my portfolio site in under an hour, I definitely spent more time debugging than generating code. And I’m no rookie. I’ve accumulated years of prompt engineering and hands-on LLM experimentation — just look at my AI project history and you’ll see how early I got into the game. Yet even with that background, I found myself walking Claude 4 around, finding more issues than fixing them in a lot of the times, pasting error logs, and iterating over prompts to narrow down the cause.

Vibe coding can feel magical, but it’s far from automatic. It still demands coding experience, software architecture, prompt engineering and significant back-and-forth with your IDE chat window.

**If Code Isn’t Written by Humans any more, What Are Programmers For?**

The value of programmers doesn’t lie in their ability to write code, but in their depth of understanding problems, the thought process of their design solutions, their ability to control context, and their skill in coordinating resources. These core abilities will not disappear because of changes in tools — on the contrary, they will become even more important.

Today’s “10x engineers” with vibe coding, can become “100x engineers” harnessing AI as a force multiplier. However, non-coders will drive straight into vibe-coded walls and face significant setbacks in no time. They’ll have to learn programming the hard way — through mistakes, this time generated by AI.

Interestingly, I believe this isn’t a bug; it’s a feature. Struggling through errors and debugging prompts is arguably the most effective way to learn coding fundamentals, especially for beginners. I laugh when people say programmers are going to be out of jobs in the future. Just like when cars were invented, people were saying there wouldn’t be any horse riders. No, they just became drivers, and there are still people who ride horses for fun.

The future won’t belong to those who fear vibe coding, nor those who blindly trust it. It will belong to those who embrace it critically — who understand when to vibe, when to dive deep, and when to step back and rebuild from scratch.

Ready to vibe?
